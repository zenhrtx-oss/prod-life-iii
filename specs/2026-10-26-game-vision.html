How the loops connect

Pillar 1 · Make

Real beats drive everything

Every system in the game grades or plays beats the player actually made. That's what separates this from the music tycoons that died: there's no way to get famous without making things.

  • BUILTFive-row drum grid (kick, snare, clap, hat, 808), hat rolls, a pitched 808, swing, three beat slots, a desk camera and a phone layout.
  • BUILTFL-style piano roll: three voices, scale lock, velocity accents and a chord stamp.
  • BUILTTAPE and IRON drum kits plus MUSIC BOX, PLUCK and CHIME voices, sold as gear. Licensed Roblox sounds only.
  • BUILTSession requests from fictional artists, daily requests, the Weekly Single, and SAMPLE IT flips of someone else's beat.
  • CHECKED"Make the beat he asked for": an artist's card sends you to the beat maker, and your stars decide whether he uses it.
  • CHECKEDSkill cards: some visitors swap the hidden roll for a 20-second task in the grid. "i need the drop at bar 4. go."
  • NEWSong mode: chain bars into an intro, a hook and a verse. This needs a beat-format migration.
  • NEWWeekly trend: one sound everybody is chasing this week (808 slides, banjo, sped-up). Beats that match it get a better viral roll.

Pillar 2 · Live

BitLife for producers

Walk-ins are the game's age-up button. A sound-alike artist walks into your room, and everyone in the server sees him, but only you get the card. You pick, the server rolls, and the room sees the result over your head.

What happens to you

  • BUILTWalk-ins: 9 sound-alike artists and 5 role visitors, about 45 cards with follow-ups, up to 8 a day.
  • CHECKEDPhone pops, offstage news ("your beat's in a dance trend"), and cards that only show up when your life fits them (rich, broke, famous, cancelled).
  • CHECKEDLife's small stuff: the weird deck (a pigeon in the lounge, "coo."), mom dropping by with food, studio mishaps, your beat leaking, your page getting hacked, scam DMs.

Stories that come back

  • BUILTFollow-ups: a choice plants a later visit where the single charts or flops, then offers a bigger choice.
  • CHECKEDDay-one payback ("album's out. track one is yours" 20 visits later), the rival's rumour, and "he left a note" when you ignore someone.

People

  • CHECKEDA bond with every artist, a Contacts tab, and DMs between visits. Artists can sign to your camp, feud with each other until you pick a side, or ghost you until a sit-down fixes it.

Arcs and memory

  • CHECKEDArcs: cancelled and the comeback, hiring a manager, taking an advance, the label deal and its dispute, a three-day tour, awards night.
  • CHECKEDMemory: a year counter on your nameplate, a story log of your career, a daily and weekly recap with a ribbon, and your discography.
  • CHECKEDEndings: retire whenever you like. The obituary card shows your real odds, your past careers stay listed, and an old collaborator remembers you in the next one.

Pillar 2 · The phone and social media

Your phone, your page, your numbers

The phone is where the game happens when nobody walks in. It buzzes on its own; while you're in the beat maker it waits with a red count. Its social app (working name CLOUT) is how you go viral, and how you get dragged.

The apps

  • CHECKEDMESSAGES: DMs from contacts, scams and offers, each one a small card with choices.
  • NEWCLOUT: your page, a feed and posting.
  • CHECKEDNEWS: offstage news, this week's trend and the charts.
  • NEWDEALS and BANK: your sponsors and label contracts, money in and out, royalties, what you owe.
  • CHECKEDSTORY: your career, one line per thing that happened.

How going viral works NEW

  1. You post. Pick one of four fixed posts. Nobody types anything, ever, because Roblox filters player text.
  2. The roll. Your Aura, your HEAT, the stars on that beat, whether it matches the weekly trend, and the fit you're wearing on camera all tilt hidden odds.
  3. The result. A normal post adds followers. WENT VIRAL puts a gold tag over your head and a jump in followers, and the superfan comes back later with the numbers. It can also go wrong: ratio'd, a clip out of context, a beat-stealing accusation. Bad enough, and it starts the cancelled arc.
  4. It snowballs. Crossing a fame tier brings new people in: the talk-show booker at 5K, the gossip page at 20K, bigger sponsors.

Real players in your feed

  • NEWFollow other producers. Their airings, hits and posts show up in your feed as fixed-format cards you can FIRE.
  • CHECKEDComments are fixed lines from fictional handles, picked by how the post went.
  • NEWCover art and profile pictures are drawn by the game from shapes and type, so nothing is ever uploaded.

Limit: inside one server this is fine. A feed that plays other players' beats across servers waits on the open "music library" licence question.

Pillar 2 · Sponsors

Status you wear, with strings attached

Sponsors pay a little, ask for things, show off your status, and leave if your aura drops.

How a deal goes

  1. A rep walks in once your Aura meets the brand's floor. BUILT
  2. He asks one question about the brand. Get it wrong and he walks. CHECKED
  3. You sign: weekly pay, one duty and a perk. NEW contract shape, CHECKED duties.
  4. Ad gigs come in. Do it straight for sure money, or make it weird for a viral gamble. CHECKED
  5. Miss a duty twice and the rep comes with a warning. Fall under the floor and you're dropped. BUILT drop, CHECKED strikes.

Tiers and perks

  • NEWLOCAL (gum, energy drinks) from Aura 50. MID (headphones, sneakers) from Aura 150 and 5K followers. BIG (watches, streaming) from Aura 300 and 20K. Example numbers.
  • NEWOne brand per category. A rival brand can try to poach you, and switching costs Aura.
  • BUILTYour sponsor's name runs on the lounge TV ticker, and some fits need a sponsor.
  • NEWThe brand's logo on your studio wall and a SPONSORED title on your nameplate.

Sponsor pay counts toward the half-of-your-money cap together with walk-ins.

What you can change NEW

THE BIGGEST TECHNICAL RISK

Twelve furnished studios in one server, on phones. The place already has 61 lights against the cap of 36. Rooms have to be lean: neon instead of lights, a part budget per room, and Roblox streaming so a phone only loads what's near. Prove it with a throwaway test (12 furnished rooms on a real phone) before building the rest.

Pillar 4 · Together

Up to 12 producers, one building

Everything works alone. More people make it louder: an audience for your beats, rivals, crews, and drama that stays on the lounge TV for minutes.

In the room

  • BUILTBeats on the monitors with a queue, FIRE, nameplates with rank, producer cards (press G), SAMPLE IT flips.
  • BUILTCypher rounds with 3 or more players (build to one brief, anonymous listen, vote, podium) and head-to-head with 2.

The room reacts

  • CHECKEDToasts and TV headlines when someone lands a hit or gets dragged, charts for this room, and visitors who greet other producers on the way in.
  • CHECKEDA jury poll: while someone's card is open, everyone nearby votes "what would you do?" and then sees what they picked.

Playing off each other

  • CHECKEDCo-produce a walk-in together, pitch a visitor someone else ignored, and every ~45 minutes a headline artist walks in for the whole room.
  • CHECKEDCrews: up to 4 producers with a tag on every nameplate and a weekly crew goal. A manager can sign another player to their label for a day.
  • NEWVisiting: walk into anyone's studio upstairs, see their plaques, FIRE their room. Join a friend's server from the phone.

On the calendar

  • NEWFriday Night Cypher at a fixed time, with Roblox's "Notify me" RSVP and an event-only nameplate frame.
  • CHECKEDLabel party weekends, awards night with a room vote, seasonal card decks, weekly charts across servers and a podium of last week's top three.

House rules. No typed text anywhere: fixed lines, fixed posts, fixed names. Votes are anonymous and never for your own beat. Another player can never take your money. Every competitive feature gets a griefing review before it ships.

New decisions this raises

Calls only you can make

  1. The name: decided. Prod Life III (26 October). Still check it isn't taken on Roblox before it goes public.
  2. Taking OK out: done. OK's credits wall, the plaques, the posters, the signs and the menus now say Prod Life III or name the house studio's artists (26 October, Milestone 9).
  3. The real studios: done. They're now The Hall and The West Suite on the signs, the door and the Career tab, and the TV recipe no longer draws the label logos (26 October).
  4. Where studios live. My pick is rooms behind doors in the shared building, which keeps every studio part of the server. The alternative is a private studio server you teleport to, which is easier on phones but lonelier.
  5. Robux. None, or cosmetics only.

Already decided and not asked again: the parody artist names stay, with the fiction switch as a fallback. Still open from before: saving the place, the light budget (which matters more now), the publish plan and the listening checks. They're all in the tracker.

The road there

Seven phases, in order

  1. 0Make it real. Save, publish privately, turn on real saves, test on a phone and with two players, play for 10 minutes, fix what breaks.
  2. 1Clean slate. Pick the name, take OK out, rename the real studios.
  3. 2The career core. BitLife waves 1 and 2, the phone, CLOUT posting, sponsor duties.
  4. 3Your room. The throwaway 12-room phone test first, then the room ladder and build mode.
  5. 4The room reacts. Toasts, TV headlines, room charts, Friday Night Cypher.
  6. 5Launch. Icon and thumbnails (you upload them, checked against Roblox's rules first), the maturity questionnaire, a friends test, then public 1.0.
  7. 6Live. A small update every week, then the rest of this page: relationships, legacy, crews, the compound, cross-server charts.
